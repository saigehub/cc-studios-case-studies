Backed by a $15 million North Miami CRA subsidy, the project serves as Phase 1 of Omega RMG's vision to transform seven contiguous acres of downtown into a live-work-play district. While Omega possessed deep community roots and a compelling local narrative, the asset faced one critical bottleneck as it neared completion: a digital infrastructure gap. This study examines how CC Studios engineered the consumer-facing architecture required to bridge that gap and execute an accelerated leasing launch. SCOPE OF ENGAGEMENT Consumer portal development · Content production · On-site embedded production · Investor-facing materials · Go-to-market strategy · District narrative architecture 01 · THE SITUATION Digital Infrastructure Gap, Not Identity Gap By late 2024, physical construction was finalizing and Omega had secured a $103 million take-out loan as the property neared its certificate of occupancy. A rich, community-centric story already existed — Omega had hosted regional job fairs, launched a 38-unit affordable housing lottery with the North Miami CRA, and earned $15 million in public subsidy. The asset did not lack an identity. It lacked a direct-to-consumer vehicle. THE BACKEND SILO Property details, pricing, and leasing mechanics existed exclusively inside AppFolio — a property management database invisible to public search engines and organic web traffic. THE MISSING CONDUIT Omega's community-first story and master-plan vision lived offline in civic meetings and press releases, with no searchable consumer touchpoint online. THE STRATEGIC CHALLENGE Without a consumer-facing digital platform, there was no scalable mechanism to capture regional rental demand, educate prospects, or convert traffic into active pre-leases. THE OPPORTUNITY A 358-unit development with CRA backing, Brightline transit proximity, Vice City Bean in the lobby, and a 7-acre master plan — all waiting for a channel to carry it. 02 · STRATEGIC OBJECTIVES Three Mandates. One Pipeline. 01 Create Narrative Infrastructure The physical asset was complete. MARKET VALIDATION · 2026 $130M The Largest HUD Multifamily Loan Ever Closed in Florida In 2026, Omega Real Estate Management Group closed a $130 million HUD 223(f) cash-out refinance on The Gardens Residences — the largest multifamily HUD loan ever approved by HUD's Southeast Region Office and the largest in Dwight Capital's history. The refinance retired the existing bridge loan, locked a 35-year fixed rate, and returned equity to fund continued district expansion. Institutional financing at this scale requires a stabilized, performing asset. The narrative infrastructure and lease-up velocity achieved during CC Studios' engagement contributed directly to the stabilization metrics that supported that outcome.
